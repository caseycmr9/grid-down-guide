# Fact-check log: garden-2-problems (data/garden-2-problems.js)
Checked 2026-11-19 by AI against the sources listed. NOT a substitute for review by a qualified person.

Articles covered: garden-problem-finder, plant-nutrient-water-problems, garden-diseases-guide, garden-pests-beneficials (all new, single pass). Source IDs (S1, T1 ...) are defined in "Sources key" at the end. **S = page actually opened and read. T = seen only in a search-result summary (page not opened or not readable), so treated as weaker.** ucanr.edu / ipm.ucanr.edu pages opened fine this time (no 403s); several guessed UC IPM URLs returned 404, and some PDFs could not be read (University of Arizona iron-chelate PDF, WSU Puyallup baking-soda PDF, UC IPM lady-beetle PDF).

## Summary
Claims checked: 132 rows in the table below. Confirmed: 99. Corrected: 17. Softened: 5. Unverified: 11 (all low-risk visual descriptions or general advice, none found wrong). The "Corrections made" table lists 31 text changes, some of which are additions or clarifications that cover several claim rows.

Biggest changes: (1) herbicide pot test now says 3 to 4 weeks, at least 20 scoops, and persistence 30 days to 3 to 5 years; (2) deer fence set to 8 ft and rabbit fence to 2 ft (cottontails) or 3 to 4 ft (jackrabbits); (3) wireworm trap method replaced (the "carrot, check every 2 to 3 days" version had no source); (4) spinosad bee wording tied to the label (about 3 hours) with "never on flowering plants or weeds in bloom"; (5) bleach-mixing warning widened to alcohol and vinegar; (6) "Do not can blighted tomatoes or potatoes" added; (7) curly top "resistant varieties" softened (UC says tomato has no true resistance); (8) clubroot rotation given as 5 to 7 years; (9) DE dust-mask and garden-grade-only note; (10) homemade dish-soap spray described as a last resort.

## Needs human expert review
- **Hardware cloth gauge.** UC IPM gives mesh (1/2-3/4 in for gophers, 1/4 in for voles) and burial depth (gopher fencing at least 2 ft deep with 6 in bent out; vole fence 6-10 in) but no gauge. The article gives no gauge. A local extension agent could add one.
- **Fire blight pruning distance.** UC IPM says cut at the next branch junction below the visible infection and that cut location matters more than tool cleaning. Many other extension sources say 8-12 in below the margin. The article follows UC IPM.
- **Late blight tubers and compost.** Penn State says discard the whole potato; UMass says cutting out the rot is as safe as for other diseases and that a hot or fully decomposed pile is acceptable. The article keeps the conservative line (no compost, no storage, no canning, eat only firm clean ones soon, hedged).
- **Spinosad and bees.** The 3-hour figure comes from label/technote language seen in search results, not a page I could open. The controlling rule (never on flowering plants) is confirmed by UC IPM. Confirm the exact wording on the product label.
- **Clubroot rotation.** UConn says 7 years once clubroot is found; a search summary of OSU said 5-6. garden-3 says 5-7, garden-5 says 4-7. Article now says 5-7.
- **Baking-soda burn and pool-grade DE statements** rest on secondary sources (ATTRA, WSU summary, garden sites, NPIC crystalline-silica note). They are conservative but should get an extension agent's nod.
- **Curly top resistance.** UC IPM home-garden page says "resistant or tolerant varieties"; the UC agriculture guideline (search summary) says tomato and pepper have no genetic resistance. Article hedges.
- **Bacterial wilt, squash vine borer, root-knot nematode, Bt, copper, sunscald, tip burn** rest on search summaries (extension pages were identified but not opened). Low risk.
- **Magnesium mobility.** Cornell lists Mg as both mobile and immobile. The article treats Mg as an old-leaf shortage (the common textbook position). Sulfur, zinc and manganese deficiencies are not in the article.
- **Regional and legal:** California gopher/vole poison-bait rules (UC IPM itself is inconsistent on zinc phosphide for residential voles), state wildlife trapping rules, the California agricultural commissioner complaint route, and whether home-use labels carry bee boxes all vary by place and product. Squash vine borer is mostly east of the Rockies.
- **Wireworm trapping.** The new method rests on USU (opened) and a Missouri Botanical Garden summary (page would not open). Spacing and timing vary by source (check after 7-14 days).
- **Length.** The validator warns that all four articles are over 1,000 words (about 1,050-1,110). Not trimmed (scope was facts); the lead may want to split or trim.
