# Fact-check log: home-antimicrobials (new guide, 2026-11-16)

Written by the lead (AI) and checked with web searches only; **not reviewed by a qualified person.** Several sources were secondary (summaries, aggregator pages), so claims are kept conservative.

## Design decision
The guide answers "how do I make antibiotics?" by explaining why it cannot be done safely and what to do instead. It deliberately contains **no procedure for culturing or extracting penicillin or any other antibiotic**: a home mold brew has an unknown dose, can contain mycotoxins and other contaminants, and is not a substitute for real treatment of serious infection.

## Claims checked
| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Household bread/fruit molds are mostly not penicillin producers; some make mycotoxins | Consistent across the sources found (secondary) | Search results (explainer and science-news pages) |

| Dose/potency of homemade batches cannot be verified; underdosing promotes resistance | Consistent across sources found (secondary) | Same |

| Garlic: lab activity, no good evidence of treating infections in people | Unverified: no sourced clinical evidence was found in the search | Needs a quick check (NCCIH garlic page) |

## Needs human expert review
- A clinician or pharmacist should read the whole guide, especially the "signs you need real antibiotics now" list and the honey advice.
- Povidone-iodine was left out on purpose because the evidence on dilution and benefit is unclear.
