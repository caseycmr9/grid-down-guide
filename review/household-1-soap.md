All soap recipe quantities were recomputed in Node (script: scratchpad h1/calc.mjs): lye = fat x SAP x (1 - superfat), water = 2 x lye (lye solution = 33.3%), lye rounded DOWN to a whole gram. Effective superfat after rounding 8.2% to 8.8%. Sensitivity check: if the true SAP values sit at the extremes of the published ranges (Wikipedia KOH-derived ranges: olive 0.1312-0.1397, lard 0.1368-0.1447, coconut 0.1768-0.1910), the real superfat stays between 6.3% and 12.8% for batches A, B and C.

## Needs human expert review
- **Flush time and vinegar.** MedlinePlus says at least 15 minutes for skin and eyes; Merck says 30 minutes or more for strong alkali eye burns; the article uses 30 minutes. NOAA CAMEO (1999 data) still lists a dilute vinegar rinse after flushing, which modern sources discourage. A nurse, EMT or poison-center specialist should confirm "30 minutes, no neutralizer" and the swallowed-lye wording (rinse mouth, sips of water or milk only if alert).
- **SAP values.** Values differ by chart (olive 0.131-0.1353, lard 0.136-0.140, coconut 0.176-0.191). A soap maker or chemist should confirm the middle values and the 8% superfat margin, and ideally make a test batch and check pH. The coconut value (0.182) is deliberately at the low end; some calculators use 0.183-0.191.
- **pH thresholds and the tongue zap test.** The 9-10 normal and 11+ lye-heavy thresholds were not confirmed on an opened page. The zap test touches a possibly caustic bar to the tongue; the article uses the fingertip method from Classic Bells and says never lick the bar, but an expert may prefer to drop it entirely.
- **Wood-ash soft soap.** No source gives weights; the 1:3 by volume starting point and the "lean fat-heavy, test and adjust" method are a synthesis of Mother Earth News and the NPS Fort Scott rule of thumb. Has not been test-made by the author. A traditional soap maker should review.
- **Lye disposal.** The "dilute at least 10 times, pour down a household drain or on bare ground far from water" advice has no source. Local rules, septic systems and wells matter.
- **Fat storage times.** Extension sources differ (Montana State: weeks at room temperature, months in the fridge, up to a year frozen; Nebraska: months at room temperature, over a year cold). Conservative figures used. A food-safety extension agent could confirm, especially for rendered fat that still contains meat bits.
- **Not rendering fat from sick animals**, and wet-versus-dry rendering temperatures (250 F) are carried over from existing app text or common practice, not checked.
- **Saponin plants.** No source gave a toxic dose. The articles tell readers never to swallow the wash and to confirm plant identity with a field guide and a person. Regional plant legality and look-alikes need local expert review.
- **Hardwood vs softwood ash.** Sources agree only qualitatively; a University of Missouri extension page was seen only in search results.
- **Not included because unverified:** potassium hydroxide flake purity (a common 90% figure appeared only in search results) and any ash-lye concentration percentage.
