## Needs human expert review
(Updated in the second pass, 2026-11-16.)
- **A licensed ham operator** should read ham-study-rules and ham-emergency-ops. The CTCSS explanation and the 70 cm offset (usually 5 MHz) are now confirmed by ARRL study materials. Still to check: the Q-signal list, the "Break, break. Emergency." wording, the radiogram preamble list, how to call on a repeater and net courtesy, and the satellite and digital-mode names.
- **Fees and dates will change.** Exam fee, the $35 FCC fee, GMRS fee, and the pool dates should be rechecked each year, and the Technician pool must be re-written for the next pool (July 1, 2030). The article tells readers to check with ARRL or the FCC.
- **GMRS "no exam" and the 18-or-older rule** are now confirmed from the rule text reproduced in FCC 17-57. The $35 fee was seen only in an FCC fee-table search result because fcc.gov blocks automated access; an expert should confirm the current fee on fcc.gov.
- **Online exam details** (camera, ID on camera) differ between VE teams. The article tells readers to ask.
- **Course times and costs** (CERT hours, Red Cross prices, hunter education hours, EMT length) are typical ranges from local pages and search results, not official national figures. A nurse, EMT or CERT coordinator should confirm they are fair. Hunter education rules differ by state.
- **Handheld buying advice** (dual-band, removable antenna, risk of out-of-band transmit on cheap radios) is general guidance and should be reviewed by someone who has bought and programmed such radios.
- **Fuse, RF exposure, lightning and tower advice** in ham-study-basics is conservative general safety advice (fuse in the positive wire near the battery, same-rating replacement). The article gives no wire-gauge or fuse-size numbers and no numeric RF exposure limits on purpose. An electrical or RF safety reviewer could check it.
- **ICS structure and component descriptions** in ham-study-basics and ham-emergency-ops are general knowledge; FEMA IS-700.b exists (confirmed) but its content was not read.
- **Book list**: titles and authors were checked against publisher or library listings in search results only. The list is suggestions, not endorsements. A librarian or subject expert should confirm editions are still in print. CoMaps is named because it forked from Organic Maps in 2025; confirm both are still maintained when the app is published.
- **Hesperian books** were written for low-resource settings. The article warns that some medicines and doses differ from US practice; a clinician should confirm that warning is strong enough.
