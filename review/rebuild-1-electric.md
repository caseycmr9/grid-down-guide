Decisions driven by sources while drafting (no earlier text existed):
- Lead-acid 100% rested voltage written as 12.65 V (Battery University BU-903), not the 12.7 V often quoted.
- Hydrogen flammable range written as about 4% to 74% (US DOE properties table, https://www.energy.gov/node/806891; some sources say 75%).
- Wire resistance values taken from Engineering ToolBox (https://engineeringtoolbox.com/copper-wire-d_1429.html) because the NEC Table 8 could not be opened and trade sites disagreed (solid 8 AWG 0.778 vs stranded 0.764).
- No percent-per-year panel degradation figure is stated, because the NREL page could not be opened.
- Equalizing is limited to flooded batteries; lithium, AGM and gel are marked "never" (Trojan User's Guide; Battery University).
- Sun-hour values are labeled cautious assumptions, not measured data.

## Needs human expert review
- **Licensed electrician (priority):** read all of electricity-basics, solar-wiring and cabin-electrical. In particular check: the 60 C ampacity table and "biggest fuse" column (secondary sources only; NEC Table 310.16 and Chapter 9 Table 8 were never opened); the inverter fuse and cable table (fuse = 1.25 x DC amps, cable at or above fuse rating, 3 ft run). The table uses nominal battery voltage, but the code method uses the lowest battery voltage the inverter will run (IAEI 2011), so for a 12 V inverter the real DC amps, fuse and cable may be one size larger; the article now says to use the manual's numbers. Also check the grounding wording. Second pass confirmed from opened sources: the string rule (125% of Isc twice, 1.56 overall; Holt guide and EC&M), the Voc cold multiplier (1.25 at -40 C; Holt guide and IAEI), the connect order (Morningstar) and the neutral-ground bond wording (Magnum manual).
- **Code edition and region:** NEC rules change by edition (the Holt guide is 2014 NEC, the PV fuse source is a 2012-era article, the inverter-input example is 2011 NEC, the GFCI source cites NEC 2020). Search results disagree on whether a battery-to-inverter circuit falls under Article 690 or Article 706 (energy storage). Adopted code varies by state and country, and wire colors differ outside the US. The 3% voltage-drop figure is advisory, not a code limit.
- **Sun-hour values** (now 1 to 2 / 2 to 3 / 4) and the 0.75 and 0.85 efficiency factors are cautious author assumptions. December city figures came from commercial web tables that disagree with each other, not from NREL tables. A solar installer or NREL PVWatts should confirm them for a given region.
- **Battery charge voltages** come from one maker (Trojan) and one reference (Battery University). Each battery's own label overrides them. Gel float and AGM float should be checked against the specific datasheet. LiFePO4 float and absorption settings vary by maker.
- **Battery acid first aid:** now sourced for eyes (NOAA CAMEO 15 minutes; NJ Dept of Health 30 minutes) and for "do not induce vomiting or neutralize" (NOAA CAMEO). Skin flush time (article: at least 15 minutes) is not given by either opened source. A poison control specialist should confirm.
- **Lithium fire guidance:** the articles say only to leave the area and call for help. No extinguishing method is given because none was verified from an opened source.
- **Salvage article:** the microwave and CRT voltage figures come from a hobbyist repair FAQ and an iFixit wiki, not a regulator. The article deliberately gives no opening, discharging or reuse instructions for these items. A technician should confirm the "what not to open" list is complete.
- **Panel testing:** the optional short-circuit amps test (small panels only) and the cell-count voltage guide should be checked by someone who tests panels.
