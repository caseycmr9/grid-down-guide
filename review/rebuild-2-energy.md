## Needs human expert review
- **Electrical (licensed electrician or experienced off-grid installer):** diode, fuse and rectifier ratings ("twice the expected current"); the three-phase shorting switch used as a turbine brake; dump-load sizing; car-alternator field wiring and the "never disconnect the battery" rule; permanent-magnet motor as a generator (reverse-current diode). Wire and fuse sizes are deferred to solar-wiring.
- **Wind towers (tower installer or structural engineer):** fall zone (tower height plus blade length is a conservative planning figure, not from a source; manufacturer manuals in search results say farther than the tower height, and ASES small-wind guidance says at least two people on site and that guyed tilt-up towers are very unstable while being raised or lowered), raising procedures, guy anchors, lightning grounding, and tilt-up safety. No source on distance from power lines was found. The article gives no anchor distances or dimensions on purpose.
- **Wind output:** the 60-110 W estimate for an 8 ft rotor at 12 mph assumes 15-25% overall efficiency (my assumption). The 91 W per m2 figure and the 9-22% capacity factor are sourced. The DOE site threshold was checked in the second pass (DOE fact sheet: 9 mph / 4 m/s at hub height; the article now says about 9 to 10 mph).
- **Shorting-switch brake:** the idea is sourced (Hugh Piggott). The strong-wind limit (a shorted alternator may not stop the rotor and can overheat the stator) comes from patent text and forum posts in search results. A wind installer should confirm the wording and add the right shutdown steps for a given machine.
- **Tip-speed ratio** 5 to 7: second pass found papers at about 5.4 (experiment), 6 (a small design) and 7 (blade-element model); the range is reasonable, and an engineer should confirm the best value for a given blade.
- **Micro-hydro:** the head x gpm / 10 rule is checked only by arithmetic against Canyon Hydro (about 53% efficiency); the DOE page could not be opened. Wheel head ranges came from a search-result summary and other sources give different bounds. PVC pressure ratings and penstock friction need the pipe maker's tables. Water rights, permits and fish-passage laws vary by state and country.
- **Biogas, wood gas, steam (specialists):** the article keeps these to concepts and strong warnings. A gas-safety or boiler-inspection professional should check the wording, especially "never build a sealed boiler without a proper safety valve and gauge". Wood-gas CO is now "often 20 to 30%" (Wikipedia table, 27%; tertiary source). Biogas yield 0.03-0.04 m3 per kg comes from one Practical Action table.
- **Human and animal power:** 75-100 W mechanical for an hour, 40-70 W electrical and hand-crank "a few watts" are still not tied to an opened source. Horse about 1 hp (The Henry Ford) and thermoelectric about 3 W (BioLite listings in search results) are now sourced.
- **Neodymium magnets (physician or medical-device safety reviewer):** the 12 in (30 cm) pacemaker distance is deliberately larger than the 8 in (20 cm) that sellers give and the 6 in (15 cm) in the FDA communication about phone and watch magnets. A cardiologist should confirm. The 175 F (80 C) rating for standard grades is from supplier datasheets seen in search results; thin magnets can fail earlier.
- **Car alternator:** field excitation and about 1,000 rpm cut-in are from forum sources only. The "never disconnect the battery while it spins" rule found no source and is kept as conservative advice.
- **Silica and flour dust:** the article now says N95 at the least and a half-mask with P100 filters is better, plus wet work outdoors. An industrial hygienist should confirm for stone dressing; the OSHA Table 1 grinding row was not opened.
- **Pump suction numbers:** the sea-level 25 ft figure and altitude table are from one handbook (opened). They are conservative for cold water, and warm water lowers them further.
