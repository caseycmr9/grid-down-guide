## Needs human expert review
- **Kidney bean boil time (resolved).** The FDA Bad Bug Book says 30 minutes at a boil (10 minutes destroyed the toxin in one study). The recipes, cooking-staples and safe-cooking-temps now all say at least 30 minutes, never a slow cooker or thermos. Checked again in the second pass.
- **Rabbit and game temperature.** foodsafety.gov (search summary) lists rabbit at 160 F; game-meat-safety and these recipes use 165 F, which agrees with CDC MMWR 2024 (opened: game meat to at least 165 F kills Trichinella). Separate conflict for the lead: the app canonical value for wild game, bear and wild pig is 160 F, but the same CDC report says 165 F. These recipes contain no bear or wild-pig dishes, so nothing here changes.
- **Pressure-cooker bean times (second pass).** Corrected using NDSU and Colorado State tests, which disagree widely (4 to 35 minutes). The kidney-bean floor of 20 minutes at pressure is a judgment from NDSU's 20-30 minute range, with "or boil 30 minutes first" as the safe alternative. An extension agent should confirm.
- **Home ORS.** WHO endorses the sugar-and-salt recipe when sachets are not available; the AAP says use commercial products only. A clinician should confirm the wording is acceptable for a grid-down handbook. Spoon accuracy matters: the text says level spoons.
- **Soaked oats and cold soaks without refrigeration.** No source gives a safe soaking time for raw oats or fine bulgur at room temperature. The articles limit it to 30 minutes-2 hours (1 hour above 90 F) and allow overnight only at 40 F or colder. A food-safety specialist should confirm.
- **Thermos cooking.** No source gives a safe hold time. Text requires food above 140 F when opened and tells readers to test their own flask. Dry beans, whole lentils, rice and wheat berries are excluded.
- **Fried rice and leftover rice.** Windows are 2 hours (1 hour above 90 F), 40 F, 3 days, reheat to 165 F. Some agencies (for example the UK NHS) say 1 hour and 1 day for cooked rice; these recipes follow the US FSIS/CDC figures but are conservative about the no-refrigerator case ("cook only what you will eat today").
- **Bitter squash.** Poison Control says discard any bitter squash; Oregon State Extension says mild bitterness is common. The more cautious rule is used.
- **Green or sprouted potatoes.** Poison Control says discard; Penn State says trim slight greening. Text discards green, bitter or heavily sprouted potatoes and allows removing small sprouts from firm ones.
- **Sprouting.** Seed sourcing (sold for sprouting) and the advice never to sprout kidney or lima beans should be confirmed. An FDA alfalfa-seed outbreak advisory was shown in search results dated 2026; readers should check any sprouting-seed recall if they can.
- **Pressure-cooker times, hand-test counts, coal counts for 400 F, congee ratio (1:8), no-knead yeast loaf ratio (500 g flour, 1/2 tsp yeast, 10-14 hours), pita hydration, bulgur cold-soak time, and poaching/frying times** are standard cooking practice, not checked against a source. Failure is a poor meal, not a safety event, except that undercooked flour or egg must be cooked through.
- **"Sit upright to eat and for 30 minutes after"** (soft-food section) is a standard aspiration precaution that was not checked; a nurse or speech therapist should confirm and say when swallowing problems need a clinician.
- **Honey dose for cough** uses the age bands in the app's common-illness-care article. AAP pages give both "2-5 mL" and the age bands; a clinician may prefer one.
- **Regional or legal matters:** backyard-egg, wild-game and raw-milk rules vary by state; the recipes avoid raw eggs and raw milk entirely.
