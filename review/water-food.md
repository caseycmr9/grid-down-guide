## Needs human expert review
- **WILD GAME TEMPERATURE CONFLICT (new, second pass).** CDC MMWR 2024 (opened, https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a4.htm) says cooking game meat to at least 165 F (74 C) kills Trichinella. The app canonical value is 160 F for wild game, bear and wild pig (safe-cooking-temps, game-meat-safety and others), kept unchanged as instructed. Bear and wild pig are the highest-risk meats (bear Trichinella nativa is freeze-resistant). The lead should decide whether to raise those rows to 165 F app-wide.
- **Cure #1** (6.25% nitrite, 1 level teaspoon per 5 lb): now confirmed from Oregon State PNW 784 (bacon recipes) and FSIS/MSU summaries (156 ppm for sausage-type products). Bacon, ham and dry-cure limits differ, so a meat-processing extension specialist should confirm the single teaspoon-per-5-lb figure is acceptable for all uses the article names (bacon, corned beef, sausage) and whether to add a weight-based rate (about 5.7 g per 2.3 kg).
- **Canning times and pressures**: every number was confirmed from NCHFP live recipe pages, but at 0-1,000 ft only for the sample tables. The USU generic altitude chart (+5/+10/+15/+20) is a fallback for single-time recipes; an extension agent should confirm it is acceptable to leave in the app.
- **Boiling altitude threshold**: CDC uses 6,500 ft, EPA uses 5,000 ft (the EPA page says "5,000 feet (1,000 meters)", which is itself inconsistent). The app keeps the canonical 6,500 ft and adds a note. Decide whether to adopt the more conservative 5,000 ft everywhere.
- **Chlorine dioxide and Cryptosporidium**: label says 4 hours; CDC pages differ ("somewhat effective" vs "effective at practical doses"). WMS 2023 text could not be opened.
- **Kidney beans**: FDA Bad Bug Book says boil at least 30 minutes; many other agencies say 10. The app now says 30 (conservative) in cook.js and recipes.js (checked again in the second pass).
- **Cassava**: boiling-alone claim was removed; no primary source on processing was opened. A nutrition or tropical-agriculture expert should check the replacement wording.
- **Rainwater, wells**: first-flush volume now stated as "at least 10 gallons per 1,000 sq ft, up to 50" from extension summaries (the PDFs could not be read); pump depth limit is softened. The 100-ft setback is the conservative end (CDC says 50 ft from tanks and leach fields). State and county codes differ.
- **Storage life claims**: dry beans, oats, pasta, white flour, cornmeal, nuts and whole-grain flour now follow USU Extension tables. Still not backed by an official source: freeze-dried foods (maker's claim, labeled), cooking oil (softened to about 1 year), lard, tallow, pemmican, ghee, butter in a crock, waxed cheese, and oiled and water-glassed eggs (labeled "not tested"). - **Calorie, protein and sodium numbers** (nutrition-basics): adult calories, pregnancy add-ons, protein 0.8 g/kg and sodium are now sourced. Still unverified: child and teen calorie rows, the 1,200-1,500 calorie emergency minimum, scurvy timing, the deficiency table, the polar bear liver warning and the pine-needle-tea caution. A dietitian should confirm those.
- **Pressure-cooker bean times** were corrected from NDSU and Colorado State tests, but extension tests disagree widely (4 to 35 minutes depending on soak and cooker).
