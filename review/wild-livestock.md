## Needs human expert review
- **Universal Edibility Test (`edibility-test`).** Second pass: a transcription of FM 21-76 chapter 9 (survivalmanual.github.io/09, not an official Army site) confirms the article's steps (8 h fast, 15 min skin, 3 min lip, 15 min tongue and chew, 8 h waits, 1/4 cup). The article adds a 15-minute "corner of mouth" step that is not in FM 21-76. The current Army manual ATP 3-50.21 uses a shorter protocol. FM 21-76 says to induce vomiting after a reaction; the article says not to, which matches poison.org and Mayo Clinic advice. A wilderness-medicine or poison-center reviewer should confirm the steps and the wording, and decide whether the test should stay in the app at all. The article already says it is a last resort and unreliable.
- **Plant and mushroom identification.** Only text is possible here. A botanist or mycologist should review the lookalike table, chicken of the woods, the identification text for every "safe" green (dandelion, plantain, lamb's quarters, wood sorrel, mallow, chickweed), root (arrowhead, Jerusalem artichoke, groundnut, kudzu) and conifer, and the cattail versus iris and burdock versus rhubarb/foxglove advice. Second pass: the morel cut-open test (corrected wording), the giant puffball test, and the ramps versus lily of the valley, autumn crocus and daffodil pairings now have agency sources, but the final identification test for each should still be shown in person. Pokeweed is kept as "avoid it" because poison-center sources report illness even after double boiling.
- **Wild game temperature.** Sources differ: USDA/FDA text I opened says wild game meat to 160 F; USDA charts for inspected beef, pork, lamb, and veal say 145 F with a 3-minute rest. I used 160 F for venison steaks and roasts (more conservative). Brucellosis "160 F" and tularemia "165 F" are conservative values; CDC pages gave no temperature. FSIS "Hunting and Food Safety" returned 403, so the USDA hunting page itself was not checked.
- **Fish parasite freezing** (-4 F for 7 days) was confirmed in the second pass against Washington State WAC 246-215-03425, which adopts FDA Food Code 3-402.11. It applies to fish served raw; most home freezers are not that cold or monitored.
- **Ice thickness** follows Minnesota DNR for new clear ice. Local conditions, currents, and snow cover change safe thickness. `nav.js` and `preserve.js` also state "4 inches"; they agree.
- **Livestock husbandry figures.** Second pass confirmed brooder temperatures, rabbit and small-ruminant gestation, sheep copper, rabbit heat stress (85 F), poultry scald limit (147 F) and American foulbrood handling against Extension pages. - **Raw milk and cheese.** I left the home-pasteurizing instructions (confirmed against 21 CFR 131.3) but kept the "just to a simmer" fallback.
